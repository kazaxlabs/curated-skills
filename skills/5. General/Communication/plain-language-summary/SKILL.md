# Plain-language summary

The owner is not a developer, an accountant or a lawyer. He needs to know what changed, what is true now, and what
he has to do, in words he would use himself.

## 1. The « En clair : » ending

**When:** every answer that describes a change (a file written, a setting changed, an account step done, a document
produced), a technical or security explanation, a review, a report, or the result of a check.
**Skip it for:** one-line factual answers, questions back to the owner, and answers with no technical content.

**Shape:** the label « En clair : » (in the owner's language; « In plain English: » for an English reader), then two to
four sentences.

**Rules:**
- No jargon, no file paths, no command names, no acronyms the owner did not use first.
- Say what changed or what is true, then why it matters to him, then what he has to do (or that he has nothing to do).
- If something failed or was not checked, say so here too. Do not soften it.
- No new information: the ending summarises the answer above it.

**Example:**

> En clair : votre profil d'entreprise est rédigé et enregistré. Les autres outils vont maintenant s'en servir au lieu
> de vous reposer les mêmes questions. Deux prix sont encore marqués « à confirmer » : il faudra me les préciser.

## 2. Plain-language rewrite of a text

**When:** the owner pastes or points to a text he finds hard (a provider's e-mail, terms of service, a technical
report, a government notice) and asks what it means, or asks for a simpler version for his customers.

**Steps:**
1. Find the purpose: what does the text want the reader to know or do?
2. Rewrite with:
   - the main point first;
   - short sentences, one idea each, active voice;
   - common words; any technical term that must stay is explained once, in brackets;
   - numbers, dates and amounts kept exactly as in the original, with their units;
   - lists for steps and conditions.
3. Add « Ce que vous devez faire » when the text asks for an action, with the deadline if the original gives one.
4. List anything you could not make plain without changing the meaning.

**Limits:**
- Legal and contractual texts: never replace the original. Give a plain explanation beside it, say that it is not
  legal advice, and point to what a lawyer or notary should check. Signing or accepting terms is always the owner's
  decision.
- Never add facts, promises or numbers that are not in the original.
- A rewrite meant for customers is checked against the compliance limits in `profil-entreprise.md` (section 11) before
  it goes out, and reviewed before it is published or sent.
